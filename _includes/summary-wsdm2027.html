About the workshop

Long-horizon information seeking has emerged as a key characteristic of information seeking systems in the agentic era. We define it at two levels.

  • At the level of a single complex query, a system may need to carry out a large number of search and reasoning rounds across multiple resources. For example, a complex user question in deep research can require over one hundred search calls.
  • Beyond individual queries, users issue sequences of related queries over multiple days or months, where later queries build on information accumulated from earlier interactions. For example, a user managing a health concern may first search for symptoms, later compare treatments, and return over months to interpret new test results.
