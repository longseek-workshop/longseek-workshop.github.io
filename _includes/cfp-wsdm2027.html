Themes and Topics

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to, the following. We are also open to other topics. Negative results and analyses of failure cases are also welcome.

Memory management over long temporal horizons

Appending all past observations (e.g., retrieved documents) and actions to the context can cause context-window overflow, introduce irrelevant or redundant information, and increase inference costs.

  • Context filtering and compression: How can long, accumulated contexts be filtered or compressed while preserving information needed for subsequent search, reasoning, and decision-making?
  • Long-term memory organization and retrieval: How can agents organize and update long-term memory, including file-based memory? How can an agent manage information flow between its long-term memory and its active context? How can agents search long-term memory to retrieve and select relevant information from long, accumulated interaction histories?
  • Consistent personalization: How can agents maintain consistent personalization across interactions while adapting to changes in users' preferences and needs?

Optimization over long temporal horizons

Step-wise supervision is scarce over long horizons, and reinforcement learning with outcome-only rewards leads to unstable training and poorly optimized intermediate steps.

  • Optimization with search calls: How can systems issue search queries and use retrieval pipelines effectively and efficiently over long horizons?
  • Intermediate supervision and synthetic data generation: How can we obtain training data or annotations for intermediate steps?
  • Learning with sparse rewards: How can systems handle delayed and sparse rewards in long-horizon processes?
  • Lifelong learning and self-evolution: How can systems continually improve from accumulated interactions and experience across queries and over time?

Evaluation over long temporal horizons

Most work evaluates only final answers, but errors accumulate over long horizons, so intermediate steps such as search quality also need evaluation.

  • Evaluation data curation with intermediate steps: What datasets can assess intermediate search results, decisions, and actions?
  • Multi-dimensional evaluation: How can we measure efficiency, latency, robustness, computational cost, and error propagation beyond final-task effectiveness?
  • Transparency and explainability: How can we measure transparency and explainability at intermediate steps and in the final answer?

Long horizons in multilingual and multimodal scenarios

Evidence spread across languages and modalities must stay aligned and consistent in memory over many steps.

  • Multilingual: How can systems align evidence across multiple languages?
  • Multimodal: How can systems reason over text, images, and other modalities with consistent representations and memory?

Long horizons in domain-specific scenarios

We welcome work on law, finance, medicine, science, education, enterprise, and other domains, including domain knowledge integration and domain-specific models and applications.

Human-centered long-horizon information tasks

How do users inspect and correct what a system remembers, and how do trust and reliance evolve over weeks of interaction? We welcome user studies, interaction design, and qualitative work.

Deployment and industrial experience

What can be learned from real deployments, system analyses, monitoring practices, hybrid architectures, success stories, failure cases, and negative results involving systems for long-horizon information tasks?

Context explosion and efficiency bottleneck

Existing studies have shown that systems struggle to process long and accumulated contexts in a long-horizon setting. Most existing studies naively append all past information (e.g., observations and actions) as context to LLMs. However, the unboundedly growing context quickly hits context window limits of LLMs, introduces substantial irrelevant or redundant information, and incurs high inference costs or performance drop.

We aim to explore research directions, including, but not limited to, the following:

  • Trajectory memorization and management. Designing memory search systems to retrieve and dynamically select relevant information from long, accumulated contexts for subsequent decision making. Specialized mechanisms can be implemented to categorize, organize, and use heterogeneous contextual information and temporal structure to streamline information processing.
  • Context filtration and compression. Developing approaches to summarize long contexts or filter out noise while preserving relevant information in either a contextualized or parametric way for the specific purpose of usage in the long-horizon system.
  • Consistent personalization. Developing methods to sustain temporally consistent user personas at scale.

Optimization for long temporal horizons

Recent studies show that many optimization issues occur over long horizons. There is a lack of explicit step-wise supervision data for long horizons. Many existing studies use reinforcement learning (RL) with outcome-only rewards that are only available at the end of the task, leading to unstable training and insufficient optimization of intermediate steps over horizons.

We aim to explore research directions, including, but not limited to, the following:

  • Optimization with search calls. Optimizing systems to effectively and efficiently issue search queries and use IR pipelines over long time horizons.
  • Intermediate supervision and synthetic data generation. Proposing methods to generate synthetic training data or annotations that provide supervision signals for intermediate steps.
  • Learning with sparse rewards. Developing methods to better handle delayed and sparse rewards in long-horizon processes.

Evaluation over long temporal horizons

Most existing work mainly evaluates final answers, while largely overlooking the quality of intermediate steps (e.g., search quality). However, in long-horizon settings, errors often accumulate over time, making intermediate behaviors an essential part of system evaluation. In addition, long-horizon settings raise evaluation challenges beyond effectiveness alone, such as latency, robustness, transparency, and explainability (for non-technical users) over extended interactions.

We aim to explore topics including, but not limited to, the following:

  • Evaluation data curation with intermediate steps. Creating evaluation datasets and assessing the quality of intermediate search results, decisions, and actions in a long-horizon process.
  • Multi-dimensional evaluation. Going beyond final-task effectiveness to consider additional dimensions such as efficiency, computational cost, and error propagation over time.
  • Transparency and explainability. Long-horizon settings make maintaining transparency and explainability more challenging. It is therefore crucial to measure transparency and explainability at both intermediate decision-making steps and the final answer.

Multilingual and multimodal scenarios

In practice, long-horizon information seeking involves interacting with information across languages and modalities over extended periods of time, introducing additional challenges for search, representation, and reasoning. These challenges grow with the horizon: evidence in different languages and modalities must be aligned and kept consistent in memory across many steps, and an alignment error made early propagates to every later decision.

We aim to explore the following directions:

  • Multilingual. Supporting long-horizon information seeking when evidence is distributed across multiple languages, requiring cross-lingual alignment.
  • Multimodal. Studying how systems can reason over text, images, and other modalities over long time horizons while maintaining consistent representations and memory.

Long horizons in domain-specific scenarios

Limited research has explored long-horizon information seeking in domain-specific settings, such as law, finance, medicine, science, and education. We encourage contributions that investigate domain-specific scenarios, including integrating domain knowledge into long-horizon systems and developing domain-specific models and applications.

Human-centered long-horizon information seeking

Long horizons also raise human-centered questions: how users inspect and correct what a system remembers, how trust and reliance evolve over extended interactions, and how to study users over weeks rather than in single sessions. We welcome user studies, interaction design, and qualitative work alongside technical contributions.

We are open to other topics, including, but not limited to, lifelong learning and self-evolving methods in long-horizon settings, as well as new resources and perspectives.
